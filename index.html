About

I'm a machine learning researcher and engineer. I completed my Master's in Computer Science at the University of Waterloo with a 4.0, supervised by Pascal Poupart, and I continue to do research at the Vector Institute as a faculty-member researcher in his group.

Before research pulled me in, I shipped software across the industry — Ethereum staking infrastructure at BitGo, region-build automation at Amazon, security analytics at IBM, and control software for quantum computing hardware at a research lab. My research interests are broad but orbit a single question: how do we get more capability out of the models and data we already have? That has meant reinforcement learning as a decision layer over pretrained models, counterfactual data augmentation for low-data regimes, probabilistic methods, and — most recently — the data problem behind computer-use agents.

Counterfactual Residual Data Augmentation for Regression

Hossein Mohebbi, Oliver Schulte, Ke Li, Pascal Poupart

Once a regressor has captured the systematic part of the data, what's left over — the residual — can be treated as reusable noise. CRDA generates counterfactual training samples by perturbing features while recycling the original residuals, expanding a dataset without collecting anything new. Average MSE drops of 22.9% for MLPs and 6.4% for XGBoost across nine datasets, ahead of five augmentation baselines.

Preprint arXiv · Nov 2025

Image-POSER: Reflective RL for Multi-Expert Image Generation and Editing

Hossein Mohebbi, Mohammed Abdulrahman, Yanting Miao, Pascal Poupart, Suraj Kothawade

No single image model is best at everything, so let an RL agent decide. Image-POSER frames image synthesis as an MDP: long prompts are decomposed into subtasks, a learned policy routes each one through a registry of pretrained generation and editing experts, and a vision-language critic supplies structured feedback as reward. The result outperforms frontier baselines on alignment, fidelity, and aesthetics — and is consistently preferred by human evaluators.

Now

■ currently in stealth

I'm building a startup — a new kind of AI lab. The premise: computer-use agents (models that read a screen, move a cursor, click, type, and switch between apps) are bottlenecked not by architecture but by a shortage of real human interaction data. We're building the platform to collect that data at scale from everyday computer work, and using it to train and improve computer-use agents — with models, benchmarks, and datasets released openly.
